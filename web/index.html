Core Research Finding

StateFrontier breaks the sequential protocol barrier

Conventional point mutations (bitflips, random replacements) fail on multi-step stateful protocols because the probability of synthesizing valid multi-token sequences decreases exponentially (P ∝ (1/256)k). Genetic crossover cannot help initially because required tokens (STEP=2) do not exist anywhere in the initial corpus.

StateFrontier indexes seeds by their deepest validated state machine depth and targets the terminal structural delimiter with grammar vocabulary. In our 55-trial ablation study, StateFrontier alone drove a +10.20 unit coverage leap and reached Depth 19 in 100% of trials in <350 iterations.

Key Research Insight: The Disproof of the Seed-Learning Hypothesis

We initially hypothesized that an online linear contextual bandit would steer seed scheduling toward high-yield inputs. In our Phase 8 ablation (Config D vs F), we found that once StateFrontier was active, the bandit yielded ΔCoverage = -0.20 ± 4.02 (statistically indistinguishable from random) while imposing a 34.3% execution throughput penalty (-300.6 exec/s) due to feature extraction and ridge regression updates. Guided by empirical data, we removed the linear bandit from the default production pipeline.
